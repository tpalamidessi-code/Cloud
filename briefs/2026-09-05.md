# Daily Peptide & GLP-1 Brief — Saturday, September 5, 2026

*First run — this one is a baseline of the current state of play. From tomorrow,
each brief covers only what changed in the prior 24 hours.*

## Bottom line

No new material developments in the last 24 hours. But three live matters define
your risk picture right now, and all three moved in the last four weeks: FDA's
September 1 warning-letter sweep against "research use only" peptide sellers,
Eli Lilly's August 12 lawsuits over retatrutide, and the FDA advisory committee's
pending recommendation on six peptides for the 503A Bulks List. The through-line
is that the RUO label has stopped working as a shield — both FDA and Lilly are now
attacking it directly.

## 🚨 Action items

1. **"Research use only" is no longer a defense worth relying on.** FDA's August 24
   letters state plainly that the label does not put a product outside drug law —
   the agency reads *intended use* from website claims, dosing directions, context,
   and what you sell alongside it (bacteriostatic water for reconstitution was cited
   as evidence). If any of your properties pair peptides with reconstitution
   supplies or dosing guidance, that is the exposure. Worth a copy audit with
   counsel this week.
2. **Retatrutide is the sharpest edge in the category.** It is not approved, FDA said
   in June it cannot lawfully be compounded, and Lilly is now suing sellers by name.
   There is no low-risk way to be in it today.
3. **Do not treat the PCAC vote as a green light.** Six peptides were *recommended*,
   not listed. Compounding them is not yet lawful.

## FDA & regulatory

**FDA posted five warning letters to online peptide sellers on September 1** — Peak
Performance Peptides, Royal Peptides, NuScience Peptides, Peptide Partners, and TXP
Innovations d/b/a Tex Peptides. The letters are dated August 24 and carry the subject
line "Unapproved New Drugs/Misbranded." Products cited across them include semaglutide,
tirzepatide, retatrutide, survodutide, mazdutide, SS-31, PT-141, tesamorelin,
tesamorelin-ipamorelin blends, and bacteriostatic water sold for reconstitution. In the
NuScience letter, FDA says the sellers' own website content caused the products to meet
the statutory definition of a drug — intended to affect the structure or function of the
body, and in some cases presented for disease uses — making them unapproved new drugs
whose sale in interstate commerce violates the FD&C Act.
*So what:* Five letters issued the same day is a coordinated sweep aimed at online
marketing, not an isolated dispute. The trigger was site copy, not the molecule.
[FDA warning letter — NuScience Peptides](https://fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/nuscience-peptides-llc-733652-08242026) · [Fierce Pharma on FDA's compounding crackdown](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-ramps-crackdown-glp-1-drug-compounders-fresh-batch-30-warning-letters)

**Advisory committee backed six peptides for the 503A Bulks List; the decision is not
final.** At its July 23–24 meeting, FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted
to recommend **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Epitalon, and Semax** for the list, and
rejected emideltide. PCAC votes are advisory only. Formal addition requires FDA action
and notice-and-comment rulemaking, which routinely runs well over a year, with HHS
sign-off above it.
*So what:* This is the single biggest potential swing for a peptide business — but the
gap between "recommended" and "lawful to compound" is measured in years, and anyone
selling into that gap now is doing so without cover.
[AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-panel-backs-6-peptides-for-compounding) · [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/fda-advisory-committee-endorses-compounding-of-certain-peptides) · [NCPA](https://ncpa.org/newsroom/qam/2026/07/31/fda-advisory-committee-nominates-six-peptides-pharmacies-compound)

**FDA is moving to permanently close large-scale GLP-1 compounding.** The agency proposed
formally excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B outsourcing
facility bulks list; the comment period closed June 29. FDA's "essentially a copy"
framework treats a compounded product as a copy where it shares the API at the same,
similar, or easily substitutable strength by the same route.
*So what:* The shortage-era window for compounded GLP-1s is being sealed by rule, not
just enforcement — a durable change, harder to litigate around than a shortage delisting.
[Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-moves-to-permanently-close-the-door-on-compounded-glp-1s) · [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/FDA-Moves-to-Shut-the-Door-on-Large-Scale-Compounding-of-GLP1-Drugs)

## Litigation & enforcement

**Lilly filed six lawsuits on August 12 over the retatrutide "black market."** Defendants:
Aesthetic Envy, Astra (Astra Peptides), Legendary Peptides, Striker Pharmacy, Texas
Peptides, and Lone Star Peptide — a mix of compounding pharmacies, med spas, and online
sellers. Lilly alleges that four of them advertised retatrutide "for research use" without
a prescription while in fact selling for human use. Lilly paired the filings with a public
call for online platforms, payment processors, and regulators to shut the market down.
*So what:* The payment-processor angle is the underrated threat. Lilly is trying to cut
off rails, which reaches sellers it never has to sue.
[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/lilly-lawsuits-obesity-drug-retatrutide.html) · [Endpoints](https://endpoints.news/eli-lilly-files-first-lawsuits-over-retatrutide-black-market/) · [Lilly investor release](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lilly-calls-online-platforms-payment-companies-and-regulators)

**Novo Nordisk's campaign is now at scale:** 132 lawsuits filed across 40 states, producing
44 permanent injunctions against sellers of compounded semaglutide. A recurring theory is
that telehealth prescribing models violate state corporate-practice-of-medicine laws by
steering patients to compounded product under a "personalized medicine" framing. Novo's
patent suit against Hims & Hers was dismissed after the two struck a commercial deal, with
Novo reserving the right to refile.
*So what:* The corporate-practice-of-medicine theory attacks the business structure, not
the product — relevant to any telehealth-adjacent model regardless of what it dispenses.
[American Med Spa Association](https://www.americanmedspa.org/news/novo-nordisk-files-14-new-lawsuits-over-compounded-semaglutide/) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/09/novo-nordisk-sues-hims-hers-compounded-obesity-drugs.html)

**Product-liability MDLs continue to grow.** MDL No. 3094 (GLP-1 receptor agonists) and a
separate NAION vision-loss MDL are both accumulating cases, driven by failure-to-warn
claims over gastrointestinal injury, blood clots, and vision loss.
*So what:* Injury litigation follows volume with a lag. It reaches sellers, not just
manufacturers, once a product is unapproved.
[Lawsuit Information Center](https://www.lawsuit-information-center.com/ozempic-naion-gastroparesis-lawsuit.html)

## Pipeline & science

**Retatrutide remains investigational.** TRIUMPH-4 showed average weight loss up to 71.2 lbs
with meaningful osteoarthritis pain relief; TRIUMPH-2 and -3 showed up to 55.8 lbs in
patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease. Roughly seven further
Phase 3 readouts are expected across 2026. US submission is expected around late 2026 to
Q1 2027, with approval realistically 2027–2028.
*So what:* Two-plus years of an approved-drug vacuum with enormous consumer demand — which
is precisely why the enforcement posture around it is this aggressive.
[Lilly](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-successful-two-additional) · [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/next-gen-weight-loss-drug-retatrutide-trial-brings-it-one-step-closer-to-fda-approval/)

## Market & business

**Direct-to-consumer GLP-1 pricing keeps falling.** As of September 4, TrumpRx lists the
Wegovy pill at $149/month, Wegovy and Ozempic pens at $199/month for the first two fills
then $349/month, and Zepbound vials at $299/month — self-pay only, government beneficiaries
excluded. Medicare began covering injectable weight-loss drugs for eligible severe-obesity
patients mid-year at roughly $245/month to the program with a $50 monthly copay cap;
about 10% of beneficiaries are expected to qualify.
*So what:* This is the quiet structural threat to the compounded market. Branded product at
$149–$299 cash erases most of the price argument that built the category.
[NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/weight-loss-drug-prices-2026-glp-1-pills-trumprx-what-expect-rcna249520) · [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/trump-announces-deals-with-eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-for-lower-weight-loss-drug-prices)
