# Daily Peptide & GLP-1 Brief — Sunday, September 6, 2026

## Bottom line

No material developments in the last 24 hours. It is a Sunday and the wires are
quiet. Three items surfaced that were not in yesterday's baseline and are worth

the April removal of 12 peptides from the "Do Not Compound" list, and confirmation

## FDA & regulatory

**FDA removed 12 peptides from Category 2 — the "Do Not Compound" list — effective
April 2026.** This preceded the July advisory committee vote and is a separate,
already-effective action. Category 2 listing flagged substances as presenting
significant safety risk; removal takes that specific barrier away, though it does
not by itself make a peptide lawful to compound.
*So what:* Worth knowing which of your molecules were on that list of 12. Coming off
Category 2 is a genuine improvement in posture, distinct from the 503A bulks-list
question still pending.
[Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/FDA-Announces-Removal-of-12-Peptides-from-Category-2-and-Schedules-PCAC-Meetings) · [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/fda-peptides-do-not-compound-list-update-2026/)

**A second peptide review is already on the calendar.** FDA has said the Pharmacy
Compounding Advisory Committee will convene again before the end of February 2027 to
review five additional peptides. Meanwhile none of the six backed in July — BPC-157,
KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax, epitalon — have been added to the 503A Bulks List, and
notice-and-comment rulemaking still has to run before any of them can be compounded.
*So what:* The pipeline is moving in a favorable direction but on a regulatory clock,
not a business one. Nothing is compoundable today that was not compoundable in July.
[AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-panel-backs-6-peptides-for-compounding) · [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/04/fdas-peptide-rally-what-compounders-and-industry-need-to-know-post-1-of-2/)

## Pipeline & science

**Compounded tirzepatide plus B12 forms a molecule that is not in the approved drug.**
A 2026 study found the two can chemically bond when compounded together; Eli Lilly
replicated the testing and stated that the clinical consequences of the new molecule
are unknown.
*So what:* This is the most substantive safety argument against compounded GLP-1s to
appear so far, and it lands squarely on the very common practice of adding B12. Expect
it in FDA rulemaking commentary and in litigation. If any product you touch pairs a
GLP-1 with B12, this is the finding to hand your counsel.
[Stanford Medicine](https://med.stanford.edu/news/insights/2026/07/glp1s-compounded-why-doctors-worry-about-safety.html)

## Litigation & enforcement

**Detail on what Lilly is actually asking for** in the six retatrutide suits filed
August 12: a permanent injunction barring each defendant from making, marketing,
distributing, dispensing, or selling anything containing or purporting to contain
retatrutide — plus disgorgement of profits and attorney's fees. Four of the six
defendants are research-use-only sellers; the others are a med spa and a compounding
pharmacy. Lilly has confirmed this is the first time it has targeted RUO sellers.
*So what:* Disgorgement means the exposure is not a licensing fee — it is every dollar
earned. That changes the calculation for anyone treating enforcement risk as a cost of
doing business.
